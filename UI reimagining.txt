

Today, the product risks feeling like:

**AI contract workspace**

I would make it feel like:

**Deal control room**

Not a dashboard. Not a document editor. Not a legal research app.

The UI should constantly answer four questions:

1. **What am I dealing with?**
2. **What can hurt me?**
3. **What should I do about it?**
4. **What happens next?**

And the interface should change as the deal moves.

---

# 1. The home screen should not be a dashboard

I would kill the conventional:

> Welcome back
> Recent audits
> Create new deal

Instead, the home screen becomes a **deal inbox**.

Think more like an email inbox crossed with Linear, but dramatically calmer.

### Top

**Your deals**

`[ Search deals ]` `+ New deal`

Then a simple stream:

```text
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Acme website redesign                     NEEDS ACTION
$5,000 · Freelance · 2h ago

3 things need your attention
Payment conflict · IP ownership · Termination

[ Review deal → ]


Nova Studio contract                     READY TO SIGN
$2,400 · Freelance · Yesterday

All previous issues resolved
No new findings

[ Open → ]


Lagos Properties                        GUARDED
₦4.2m · Purchase · Sep 12

2 obligations being watched
Next notice: Oct 31

[ Open → ]

────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

The status isn't **“analysis complete.”**

It is:

* **Needs action**
* **Negotiating**
* **Ready to sign**
* **Signed**
* **Guarded**
* **Needs attention**

That is much closer to the user's actual mental model.

When I open a deal, I don't want a generic workspace.

I want something that immediately tells me:

# 3. Findings should become a negotiation surface

This is where I think Dealenz can look genuinely different.

Don't give me:

> Risk score 73
> Severity: High
> Explanation...

That is generic AI/legal software.

Instead:

### “3 things I'd push back on”

Then each issue becomes a compact decision card.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐

PAYMENT TERMS                              HIGH

You're asked to invoice on delivery,
but another clause starts the 30-day clock
from client approval.

That can delay payment.

WHY THIS MATTERS
Your payment date is not actually defined.

WHAT TO SAY

“Payment will be due within 30 days of
invoice date, regardless of internal
approval processes.”

[ Copy ]

[ Show evidence ]

[ Ask about this ]

──────────────────────────────────────────────
```

This is where the product earns its money.

Not “here's an AI explanation.”

**Here's the problem and here's what you can actually say.**

---

# 4. Make the interface visually distinguish evidence from interpretation

I would introduce a very strong visual grammar.

### Evidence

Neutral.

> “Payment will be made within 30 days of approval.”

### Dealenz interpretation

A separate layer.

> **This creates uncertainty about when the 30-day period begins.**

### Suggested action

Another layer.

> **Ask them to anchor payment to invoice date.**

That distinction should be visible everywhere.

Something like:

```text
DOCUMENT
────────
“within 30 days of approval”

DEALENZ
────────
Approval is undefined.

ACTION
────────
Anchor payment to invoice date.

CONFIDENCE
────────
Exact clause
```

This reinforces one of Dealenz's strongest technical principles:

**AI proposes. Evidence grounds. Rules determine. You decide.**

# 5. The biggest new screen: “Push back”

I would make this a first-class destination.

Not a modal.

Not an Ask box.

Not a chat.

## PUSH BACK

```text
PAYMENT TERMS

Problem
The payment clock starts after approval.

Your position
Payment should start from invoice date.

Suggested wording

“Payment shall be due within 30 days
following receipt of the applicable invoice.”

────────────────────────────────────────

Why this wording

• removes approval dependency
• keeps the existing commercial arrangement
• gives both parties a clear date

────────────────────────────────────────

[ Copy wording ]

[ Make it softer ]

[ Make it firmer ]

[ Ask Dealenz ]

```

That is much more useful than chat.

And notice something important:

**The user doesn't need to prompt the AI.**

Dealenz already knows what needs attention.

---

# 6. Then make “Re-check” feel like a visual transformation

This could become one of the signature interactions.

After the user gets a revised contract:

### Before

```text
6 OPEN ISSUES
```

Upload/paste new version.

Then:

```text
RE-CHECKING

Comparing the new version with
your previous review...

Payment terms       RESOLVED
Scope               STILL OPEN
IP                  STILL OPEN
Termination         NEW
```

Then:

# **1 resolved · 5 still open · 1 new**

But I'd make that verdict enormous.

And below it:

```text
WHAT CHANGED

✓ Payment conflict resolved

! Termination clause introduced
  in the new version

→ Scope remains ambiguous
```

This is a much stronger product moment than “Analysis complete.”

# 7. Signing should feel like crossing a finish line

I would radically simplify the signing experience.

Once the user reaches:

**Ready to sign**

the UI should stop behaving like an analysis application.

It becomes:

```text
READY TO SIGN

Acme Website Agreement

✓ Payment clarified
✓ Scope confirmed
✓ IP reviewed
✓ Termination reviewed

────────────────────────────

3 things you'll want to remember

Payment: 30 days from invoice
Renewal: none
Notice: 30 days

────────────────────────────

         [ SIGN DEAL ]

```

The key transition is:

**analysis → certainty → action**

This is where the product can become much more interesting.

The deal should not disappear into history.

It becomes:

# GUARDED

```text
ACME WEBSITE REDESIGN

────────────────────────────

NEXT THINGS THAT MATTER

OCT 04
Invoice due

NOV 12
Final delivery
```

That creates the second act naturally.

# 9. Counterparty memory should appear as context, not a database

I would avoid a giant “Contacts” or “Counterparties” section.

When I'm reviewing a new contract from Acme:

```text
ACME STUDIO

You've dealt with them before.

Previous deal
Website redesign · $3,500

You pushed back on:
• payment timing
• IP ownership

```

That's enough.

The product feels intelligent without feeling like CRM software.

No:

**+ New deal**

Huge composer:

```text
┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                           │
│ Paste a contract, upload a file,          │
│ or describe the deal.                     │
│                                           │
│                                           │

or

[ Upload file ]     [ Paste text ]

───────────────────────────────────────────

What are you trying to do?

○ Review their contract
○ Compare a revised version
○ Get ready to sign
○ Check an existing deal

```

Actually, I'd probably test removing even that last classifier.

Let the system infer it.

The user should be able to dump something into Dealenz and say:

> “They sent me this. Anything I should worry about?”

That's the product.

# 12. The visual language

I would move away from “legal SaaS.”

No blue enterprise dashboards.

No purple AI gradients.

No giant AI sparkle iconography.

No card-grid-everything.

No excessive badges.

No faux legal-document aesthetic.

I'd aim for:

### **Editorial + operational**

Think:

* strong typography
* large numbers
* lots of negative space
* thin structural rules
* restrained borders
* dense but readable evidence
* almost no decorative UI
* very deliberate use of one accent color
* large state changes
* subtle motion only when state changes

The product should feel **expensive because it is calm**, not because it is visually complicated.

---

# 13. I would introduce a “deal state” visual system

Not colors everywhere.

A single visual language:

### NEEDS ACTION

Large unresolved state.

### NEGOTIATING

Movement/change state.

### READY TO SIGN

Completion state.

### SIGNED

Historical state.

### GUARDED

Protected/ongoing state.

And every screen should answer:

> **What state is this deal in?**

That gives the whole application coherence.

# The biggest conceptual change

I would organize Dealenz around **moments**, not features.

### Moment 1

**They sent me something.**

> “What am I looking at?”

### Moment 2

**Something looks wrong.**

> “Why?”

### Moment 3

**I need to respond.**

> “What do I say?”

### Moment 4

**They sent it back.**

> “Did they actually fix it?”

### Moment 5

**We're signing.**

> “What am I agreeing to?”

### Moment 6

**It's signed.**

> “What do I need to remember?”

### Moment 7

**Another deal arrives.**

> “You dealt with this counterparty before.”

That is the product.

## If I were actually taking ownership

I would **not start by redesigning every screen**.

I'd establish one new visual/product system around this sequence:

```text
                    DEALENZ

                    THEIR PAPER
                         ↓
                  WHAT MATTERS
                         ↓
                   WHAT TO SAY
                         ↓
                    RE-CHECK
                         ↓
                    SIGN HERE
                         ↓
                     GUARDED
                         ↓
                  NEXT DEAL
```

Then I'd redesign only the five surfaces that make that loop tangible:

1. **Deals home**
2. **Deal overview**
3. **Finding / Push Back**
4. **Re-check**
5. **Signed / Guarded**

Everything else gets pulled toward those.

And I would be willing to throw away a surprising amount of the current UI if it doesn't strengthen that loop.

**The underlying Dealenz architecture is already sophisticated. The UI should stop trying to expose that sophistication. It should make the user feel one thing: *“I know what to do next.”***
